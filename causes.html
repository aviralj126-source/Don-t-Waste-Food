Why Does So Much Food Get Wasted? 🛒

Almost nobody throws away good food on purpose. Most food waste happens because of sneaky marketing, busy schedules, and everyday habits we don't even think about.

🔬 What I Observed in My Research

When I started this project, I thought most food was wasted by grocery stores throwing away unsold stock. But according to the UNEP 2024 report, over 60% of consumer waste actually happens inside regular homes! When I checked our family kitchen, I found half-empty bottles of sauces, wrinkled apples hidden behind yogurt pots, and leftover bread that had grown mold. It was an eye-opener.

🛍️

1. Overbuying & Bulk Deals

Supermarkets often use "Buy 2 Get 1 Free" or giant family-size bundles to make us buy more than we actually need. We feel like we got a bargain, but if the third item rots before we eat it, we actually lost money.

💡 Student tip: Always check what is already in your kitchen before shopping!
🧊

2. The "Back of the Fridge" Void

Out of sight, out of mind! Leftover soup, fresh greens, or opened cheese get pushed into the cold, dark corners behind milk cartons. By the time someone digs them out two weeks later, they are spoiled and moldy.

💡 Student tip: Keep leftovers on the front middle shelf where you can see them.
🏷️

3. Expiry Date Confusion

Millions of people treat "Best Before" dates as strict deadlines. In reality, "Best Before" only describes peak flavor and crunch! Throwing away canned beans, dry pasta, or biscuits on that exact day is completely unnecessary.

💡 Student tip: Use your eyes and nose! "Best Before" ≠ dangerous.
🍱

4. Buffets & Huge Portions

At wedding receptions, hotel buffets, and birthday parties, caterers cook massive amounts to ensure food never runs short. Guests also pile their plates with giant mounds of food they cannot finish, leaving plates half-eaten.

💡 Student tip: Start with small servings. You can always go back for seconds!
🥕

5. Rejecting "Ugly" Produce

Many grocery stores reject crooked carrots, bumpy apples, or twin strawberries because people only pick shiny, symmetrical fruits. Farmers end up tossing millions of tonnes of delicious, nutritious produce straight onto fields or trash heaps.

💡 Student tip: Weird-looking fruits and vegetables taste just as good!
🥘

6. Cooking Without a Leftover Plan

We often cook 4 portions when only 2 people are eating, but nobody wants to eat the same meal two nights in a row. Without a quick idea to turn leftovers into a new dish (like turning rice into fried rice), it sits until it gets tossed.

💡 Student tip: Label containers with the day they were cooked.
