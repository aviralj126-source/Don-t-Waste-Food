Understanding Food Dates: Stop Throwing Good Food Away! 🏷️

One of the biggest reasons families toss unopened boxes is confusing "Best Before" with "Use By". Here is how to tell them apart:

Label What It Actually Means What You Should Do
"Best Before"
(Quality)		 Refers to maximum freshness, crunch, and aroma. The food is usually completely safe to eat after this date! Don't throw it out! Check with your senses: does it look fine? Smell fine? Taste fine? If yes, it's good to eat. (e.g. crackers, pasta, canned soup).
"Use By"
(Safety)		 Refers to microbiological food safety. Used on rapidly perishable foods like raw meat, fresh dairy, and fish. Strictly follow this date! Do not eat meat or fish past its "Use By" date unless you froze it before that date arrived.
"Display Until / Sell By"
(Store Only)		 An inventory code for supermarket shelf-stockers to know when to rotate items. Ignore this label completely! It has nothing to do with food safety for customers at home.

🍳 Student Kitchen: Turn Leftovers into Gold

Don't toss yesterday's leftovers — turn them into quick, delicious snacks:

🍚

Crispy Fried Rice

Cold, day-old cooked rice is actually better than fresh rice for frying because it won't get soggy! Sauté diced leftover vegetables, soy sauce, and an egg in a hot pan for 5 minutes.

Uses: Leftover cooked rice & vegetable odds and ends.
🍌

Brown Banana Pancakes

When bananas turn brown and speckled, they are at their sweetest! Mash them with a fork, mix with flour or oats and a splash of milk, and fry into golden, naturally sweet pancakes.

Uses: Overripe spotty bananas.
🍞

Crunchy Garlic Croutons

Dry or stale bread can be diced into cubes, tossed with a spoonful of olive oil or butter, a pinch of garlic powder and salt, and baked for 8 minutes until golden and crunchy for soup or salad.

Uses: Hard or stale bread slices.
🍲

Veggie Scrap Broth

Keep a clean container in your freezer for vegetable cut-offs: carrot peels, celery leaves, onion skins, and mushroom stems. When the container is full, simmer in water for 35 mins for homemade veggie stock!

Uses: Clean vegetable peels and trims.

🌱 Composting & Giving Back

Composting at Home or School: If food is truly inedible (like fruit skins, tea leaves, or coffee grounds), never send it to the landfill! Composting allows aerobic bacteria to break it down into rich, black organic soil that feeds garden plants without generating methane gas.

Donating Untouched Food: Many local organizations, food banks, and community fridges accept packaged goods or surplus catering food. If your school has an event with leftover packaged food, ask the organizers to connect with a local charity!
